Why Eddie Chang expects generative brain models within a year

Chang gives two reasons for the short timeline: his lab and other groups are already working on the problem, and he thinks the pace of new data will determine when the first models appear. The interview explains why speech is a tractable place to start: electrodes can record a word-planning signal before the person speaks. Forecast transcript notes

Science found a measurable signal before speech

Silva, Leonard and colleagues found a subregion within Wernicke’s area, the left posterior superior temporal gyrus (lpSTG), that showed activity about 250 milliseconds before speech onset. The activity tracked properties of the upcoming word, including its length, syllable count and frequency in English. The team distinguished the anticipatory signal from nearby responses to hearing speech and from activity tied to individual speech sounds or mouth movements. Silva et al., Science, November 2026 · UCSF study summary

Direct recordings in awake participants gave the researchers the time resolution to distinguish these stages. Cortical stimulation and lesion analysis added evidence that the site plays a causal role in speech production. The researchers also decoded attempted words from activity in and around the region in two people with severe paralysis who could no longer speak. This gives BCI teams a candidate signal upstream of articulation that they can test alongside motor-area signals. Science paper · UCSF study summary

Nature Neuroscience showed speech and gesture working together

The paper linked with the interview request is Simultaneous speech and gesture decoding for multimodal communication in paralysis by Samantha C. Brosler, Jessie R. Liu, Alexander B. Silva and colleagues, with Chang as senior author. The team studied three participants with paralysis. Each participant’s sensorimotor activity was recorded with a single high-density electrocorticography (ECoG) implant, an electrode grid on the surface of the cortex. Two participants used an avatar in real-time tasks. Parallel models turned attempted speech into text and attempted gestures into avatar movements. Brosler et al., Nature Neuroscience, September 2026

How the evidence supports Chang’s forecast

Chang explicitly cites active work across research groups and the speed of data collection. The case below connects that reasoning to published results. It is an inference about feasibility, since he gives no test for what the first model must do. Transcript notes, 13:29–14:23

  1. The neural signal can lead the behavior. Speech gives researchers a tractable starting point. They can align brain activity with attempted or actual speech, distinguish planning from feedback, and test what the earlier signal adds on held-out trials. Silva et al.
  2. There are working decoders to build on. In 2025, the lab streamed personalized synthetic voice from one participant’s cortical recordings, processing neural data in 80-millisecond increments. The avatar work then showed how one implant could drive parallel outputs, with some transfer to unseen pairings. Littlejohn et al. · Brosler et al.
  3. Data are the schedule risk. Chang points to how fast recordings become available. The linked study offers a concrete example. Its replication code and figure data are public, while access to the de-identified clinical ECoG recordings requires approval. For a broader model, coverage across people, electrode placements and real-world contexts matters alongside recording time. Transcript notes · Paper and data availability

My reading of “arrival.” A credible first result would forecast a defined circuit’s future activity and related behavior from recent neural recordings plus context. The papers show that specific behaviors can already be decoded, and the Science results give one way to identify a circuit’s contribution experimentally. Generalization to new people, spontaneous tasks, and responses to previously untested interventions would each require their own evidence. For stimulation or medication, prediction from observational records alone can confound treatment effects with the reasons a person received treatment.

Earlier work helps put the date in perspective. BrainLM already forecast activity recorded with functional MRI in a 2024 study. The capability to watch for in Chang’s context is a richer connection between fast neural dynamics, behavior and external inputs. Neither 2026 paper estimates a 12-month timeline. BrainLM, ICLR 2024 · Chang’s forecast in the transcript

Transcript notes

Timestamped paraphrases of Eddie Chang’s answers, based on Real Alpha’s machine transcription of the November 26, 2026 MTS interview. The short quotation retains his qualification on timing.

3:59–5:57 · Direct recordings and word planning

Asked how the discovery was possible, Chang describes years of direct electrode recordings in patients receiving care for epilepsy or brain tumors. Asked how he distinguished planning from hearing one’s own voice, he contrasts the roughly 150-millisecond delay he sees when people listen with activity in one patch about 250 milliseconds before speech. He says the signal reflects syllable count and how frequently a word is used. Watch from 3:59 · Watch from 5:28

9:14–11:22 · Where a speech interface could record

Chang recalls the 2021 speech decoding demonstration, then distinguishes people who can still form speech motor commands from people with damage to the brain area that issues those commands. When asked if the new finding could expand access, he says his team hopes to record from both the planning and execution areas. Watch from 9:14 · Watch from 11:22

12:49–14:23 · What he means by a model and why timing depends on data

Chang describes the previous decade as device-focused and expects newer companies to give modeling more attention. Asked what that means, he describes a mathematical relationship between brain activity and behavior. Asked when an accurate model might be possible, he says many groups are already working on it and the pace depends on when the data become available. His estimate is that models will start to appear “probably within the next 12 months.” Watch from 13:29 · Watch the forecast

15:22–16:20 · What the models might predict

Chang imagines predicting how brain activity changes during conversation or in response to the environment, stimulation and medication. When asked about depression and chronic pain, he describes understanding the neural signals well enough to choose when and how strongly to stimulate as an open question. Watch from 15:22

16:41–17:35 · Partial brain models and embodiment

Asked what brain–computer interfaces might do by 2030, Chang predicts systems that model parts of the brain. He describes a participant controlling a full-body digital avatar as an example of embodiment and mentions robotics as a possible later direction. Watch from 16:41

What’s Next

Watch for a model tested on a new participant, a later session and a new context. Ask whether the neural input adds predictive value over a model given only the prompts and conversational context. For a claim about stimulation, look for a prediction registered before the intervention and checked against a suitable control. That would make “world model” a claim the evidence can test.
